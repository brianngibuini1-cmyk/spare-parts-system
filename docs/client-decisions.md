# Client decision register

This register captures unresolved product and operating policies found in the requirements, architecture, database, API, security, and implementation documents. **No recommendation below is a client decision.** A suggested default is a discussion starting point only; the owner must confirm it before behavior is encoded. “Blocks” means it blocks the dependent feature or production release, not necessarily all unrelated foundation work.

## Priority 0: decisions that block core transaction design

| ID | Decision / question | Why it matters | Blocks implementation? | Suggested default for discussion | Client must confirm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | What is the shop's base currency, and should each record carry a currency code? | Monetary columns, formatting, receipt/report totals, and rounding depend on currency and scale. Kenyan context alone does not establish KES. | Yes: product pricing, sales, purchases, receipts, and monetary reports. | One configured shop currency; KES only if confirmed. Store currency explicitly with posted monetary documents. | Currency, decimal precision, and whether multi-currency is out of scope. |
| D-02 | Are prices tax-exclusive or tax-inclusive? Does VAT/tax apply, at what rates, and what tax details must receipts show? | Changes calculation, rounding, historical line snapshots, reports, and legal receipt content. | Yes: sale calculations, receipt format, and tax-related reports. | Keep tax disabled until the owner's tax treatment and applicable obligations are confirmed. This is not a legal determination. | Applicability, rate(s), price convention, rounding, tax identifiers/wording, and whether a tax professional should validate. |
| D-03 | Which tender/payment methods are accepted, and can a transaction split across methods? | Determines checkout payload, payment records, reconciliation information, receipt, and reports. | Yes: checkout finalization and payment reporting. | Record only manually confirmed methods; do not integrate a payment provider. M-PESA remains excluded absent separate approval. | Exact methods, split tender, payment evidence/reference fields, and whether payment detail is needed at all. |
| D-04 | Are credit sales allowed? If yes, what does “paid”, “part-paid”, and “due” mean, and is customer/account tracking needed? | Credit introduces customer identity, balances, collections, payment allocation, and potentially accounting-like scope. | Yes: checkout and sales schema; otherwise implement only fully settled sales after other payment rules. | Exclude credit in v1 unless explicitly required and scoped. | Whether credit is allowed, customer fields, due tracking, part payments, limits, and reports. |
| D-05 | Are discounts allowed? Per line, whole sale, or both; fixed amount or percentage; who may apply them and what limits apply? | Impacts server-side totals, tax order, audit data, permissions, receipt and margin reporting. | Yes for discounted checkout; undiscounted checkout can proceed only if owner explicitly chooses no discount in v1. | No discounts initially unless specified; if enabled, require a reason and audit the before/after amounts. | Discount forms, limits, authorization, stacking, tax calculation order and receipt display. |
| D-06 | What is the sale finalization and stock shortage policy? Can stock go below zero? | Defines transaction rejection, concurrency checks, displayed stock, backorders and ledger validity. | Yes: sale posting and inventory consistency. | Prevent overselling by rejecting a sale that cannot be fulfilled from available stock. | Whether negative stock/backorders are permitted; reservation behavior; whether zero stock is sellable. |
| D-07 | What constitutes a return, exchange, refund, cancellation or void, and who may perform it? | Each can reverse money and/or stock differently; immutable history requires linked compensating events. Exchanges may combine return and new sale. | Yes: correction workflow and lifecycle; basic completed-sale workflow can begin if corrections are excluded from v1 explicitly. | Keep posted sales immutable; use linked reversal/return events with reason and actor. Do not delete/edit posted sales. | Supported cases, time limits, refund method, restock condition, partial returns, exchange representation, authorization and receipt/audit requirements. |
| D-08 | Which additional identifiers beyond the internal product reference are needed (for example, barcode or aliases)? | Future checkout scanning/imports may depend on extra identifiers. | No for the TASK 008 catalogue scope; yes before implementing barcode workflows. | TASK 008 confirms a generated unique internal reference and optional manufacturer part number; manufacturer part numbers and names may duplicate. | Whether barcode scanning or other aliases are needed later. |
| D-09 | What are product units and quantity precision? Are parts always whole units? Are variants/vehicle fitment required? | Affects quantity types, checkout, movement validation, product lookup and schema. | Yes: inventory and sales quantity model. | Whole-unit quantity if all stock is discrete; do not assume this without confirmation. | Unit list, fractional quantities, packaging/conversion, variants, compatibility fields and search needs. |
| D-10 | How will opening stock be established, and when does it become official? | First balances need traceable provenance; an arbitrary opening quantity can make every future balance untrustworthy. | Yes: inventory launch and any sales before first supplier receipt. | Explicit opening-stock count/import, one opening movement per item, timestamp, actor, and source note; require a reconciliation sign-off. | Cutover date, who counts, treatment of unknown quantities, import format, review/approval and whether opening value/cost is captured. |
| D-11 | What stock adjustments are allowed, for what reasons, and with what review/approval? Who can request and post one? | Prevents unrestricted edits and establishes permissions, audit, count variance and ledger movement workflow. | Yes: adjustment endpoint/UI and count workflow. Basic receipt/sale ledger can proceed if adjustments remain unavailable. | No direct balance editing; adjustment is a compensating ledger movement requiring reason and audit. Owner-only in single-user v1 is a possible starting point, not policy. | Allowed reasons, evidence, actor/approver, thresholds, count process, correction timing and whether owner confirmation is needed per adjustment. |
| D-12 | What supplier-purchase workflow is required: direct receipt, purchase order then receipt, partial deliveries, returns, costs and invoice references? | Determines purchase statuses, line quantities, receiving UI, ledger timing and cost model. | Yes: purchase/receiving implementation. Product/sales foundation can proceed independently. | Create a purchase/receipt record and add stock only when received; permit partial receipt only if explicitly confirmed. | Required lifecycle, partial receipts, supplier invoice/reference, costs, damaged/returned goods, edits/cancellation after receipt, and supplier balances (if any). |

## Priority 1: user-visible behavior and security setup

| ID | Decision / question | Why it matters | Blocks implementation? | Suggested default for discussion | Client must confirm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-13 | What must a receipt show, and what paper/printing setup is expected? | Browser print layout, legal details, customer data, unique reference and printer width vary. | Blocks final receipt implementation; not core sale storage. | Print-friendly browser page with shop identity, unique sale reference, timestamp, items, quantities, unit prices, totals and payment only as approved; no direct printer integration. | Business/legal text, tax identifiers, logo/contact details, paper size (A4/thermal width), customer details, copies and whether receipt can be reprinted. |
| D-14 | What currency, price display and future price-history/cost fields are required? | Currency and scale affect price display and eventual sale snapshots; cost/margin choices affect purchasing and reports. | Blocks sales, tax and valuation behavior, but not the TASK 008 approval workflow. | TASK 008 confirms the owner sets an approved selling price. The current schema uses `NUMERIC(18,4)` without currency or tax configuration; this precision is a technical provisional choice, not a currency decision. | Currency, display precision, rounding, cost tracking, landed costs, valuation method, and price-change history policy. |
| D-15 | What is the sale/reference numbering rule? | Human-readable references appear in receipts, search and support records. | Blocks receipt finalization; internal database IDs support earlier work. | Unique, non-reused, server-generated references; exact format can be chosen once owner confirms operational/legal expectations. | Prefix, date/year component, reset policy, gaps allowed, and legal numbering constraints. |
| D-16 | Which exact dashboard and report metrics, date ranges, exports and timezone are wanted? | Metric names can have ambiguous meanings (e.g. sales vs cash received); aggregates depend on policy and time boundaries. | Blocks final dashboard/report behavior; basic data capture can proceed. | Shop-local timezone for display, UTC storage; no accounting statements. | Timezone, date defaults, gross/net definitions, status inclusion, low-stock threshold, grouping, export format and retention. |
| D-17 | What supplier/contact and customer fields are required? Is customer capture optional or mandatory? | Data minimization, privacy, duplicate detection, receipts and credit all depend on fields. | Blocks final supplier/customer forms; customer capture may be omitted if not needed. | Store minimum supplier contact details; no customer record unless a confirmed workflow needs it. | Required supplier fields, customer fields/use, duplicate rules, privacy/retention expectations. |
| D-18 | Are roles beyond owner and product-submission attendant needed, and what permissions apply to future workflows? | Additional roles affect authorization for sales, receiving, inventory, returns and reports. | No for the TASK 008 product scope; yes before implementing other operational workflows. | TASK 008 confirms owner and attendant roles for catalogue actions. Other workflow permissions remain undecided. | Permissions for sales, receiving, adjustments, refunds, reports, owner recovery, and concurrent sessions. |
| D-19 | How is the first owner account created and recovered? Is MFA required? | A remotely reachable app needs secure initial enrollment, password reset, and lost-device recovery without a default password. | Blocks production authentication launch; local auth foundation can be built around a selected flow. | One-time setup, disabled once claimed; MFA is a security decision for owner, with no hard-coded credentials. | Bootstrap channel, verified contact method, MFA requirement, reset procedure, support contact and lockout/recovery rules. |
| D-20 | What session duration and device/session management are acceptable? | Balances shop convenience with risk from a lost phone/computer; affects cookie/session storage and revocation. | Blocks production auth configuration; not domain model. | Secure server-revocable sessions with finite idle and absolute lifetime; exact periods to be chosen. | Idle/absolute expiry, remember-me, session list/revoke behavior, and whether concurrent sessions are allowed. |
| D-21 | What audit records may be viewed/exported, who can access them, and how long are they retained? | Audit storage may contain business/personal data and is needed for investigations; retention has cost and privacy implications. | Blocks production retention/access policy; baseline event capture can start with data minimization. | Append-only from ordinary application actions; owner-only access; retention set after applicable obligations are checked. | Retention, export, access, required fields, reason requirements and any legal/accounting retention obligations. |

## Priority 2: delivery and operating decisions

| ID | Decision / question | Why it matters | Blocks implementation? | Suggested default for discussion | Client must confirm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-22 | Which cloud provider/region, domain, budget and operational owner? | Determines hosting, managed PostgreSQL, secret storage, TLS, backups, support and cost. | Blocks deployment, not local feature development. | Use a reputable managed app/database platform in an owner-approved region; choose only after cost and recovery needs are agreed. | Provider, region/data location, domain, monthly budget, account owner, deployment access and support owner. |
| D-23 | What backup frequency/retention and recovery point/time objectives are required? | “Backups enabled” does not specify acceptable loss or outage; restore capacity must be planned. | Blocks production launch. | Automated encrypted backups plus periodic restore drills; set RPO/RTO based on shop tolerance. | Maximum data loss (RPO), restoration time (RTO), retention, restore authorization and drill cadence. |
| D-24 | What availability, monitoring, alerting and support response are expected? | Internet-accessible business records need an owner when service or backup fails; paid monitoring may conflict with budget. | Blocks production operations plan, not code foundation. | Basic provider health/backup alerts and application error monitoring; avoid paid add-ons until agreed. | Availability target, alert recipient, monitoring budget, response hours, maintenance windows and incident contact. |
| D-25 | What are record retention, deletion and archive expectations? | Sales, audit, supplier and user records are linked; deletion can conflict with history and local obligations. | Blocks production retention implementation and destructive operations. | Deactivate referenced products/suppliers; avoid hard deletion of posted history. | Retention period, archive/export needs, privacy requests and deletion policy after obligations are checked. |
| D-26 | What accessibility, language, browser and print-device support is required? | Responsive design alone does not settle supported browsers, readability, translations or printer compatibility. | Does not block core backend; blocks acceptance criteria for UI/receipt. | Modern evergreen desktop/mobile browsers, English UI, keyboard-friendly forms and browser print, subject to confirmation. | Browser/device matrix, language, accessibility needs, receipt printer model/width and acceptance criteria. |

## Suggested decision order

Decide D-01 through D-12 before building checkout, product, inventory, or receiving schemas. Decide D-13 through D-21 before finalizing receipt, reporting, permissions, and production authentication. Decide D-22 through D-26 before production launch. The client may explicitly defer a feature; record that scope choice rather than silently guessing its policy.

## Additional BMM Auto Motors platform decisions

These decisions were added when the client approved an installable Flutter application for Windows, Android, and iOS. They do not change the shop's business policies above.

| ID | Decision / question | Why it matters | Blocks implementation? | Suggested default for discussion | Client must confirm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-27 | Which Windows receipt printer will be used, and how is it connected? | PDF/Windows-driver printing is broadly compatible; direct ESC/POS can control thermal paper/cutters but is model/interface-specific. | Blocks reliable printer integration and acceptance testing; not Flutter foundation. | Start with PDF/Windows print dialog. Add direct ESC/POS only if required and verified on the supplied printer. | Make/model, Windows driver, USB/network/Bluetooth interface, paper width, cutter, logo/barcode needs, and direct-vs-dialog preference. |
| D-28 | How should the Windows application be installed, signed, updated and recovered? | MSIX/store/installer packaging and code signing affect SmartScreen prompts, administrator rights and update flow. | Blocks shop deployment; not development. | Use a signed Windows installer/MSIX with a controlled update process after device policy is known. | Shop PC administrator access, managed device restrictions, installation/update method, code-signing responsibility and budget. |
| D-29 | Which hosted macOS provider/Mac will build iOS artifacts, and what monthly budget is approved? | Windows cannot build/sign iOS apps locally; provider choice controls cost, source access, signing secrets and artifact retention. | Blocks signed iOS builds/TestFlight, not Android/Windows development. | Start with low-volume usage on a protected hosted macOS runner; recheck current rate cards before enabling billing. | Provider, repository privacy, allowed spend, billing owner, region, retention, secret access and whether a physical Mac is available. |
| D-30 | Who owns the Apple Developer account and bundle identifier? | TestFlight signing and release authority should remain with the business/account owner. | Blocks TestFlight distribution; not Flutter/iOS source development. | Business owner enrolls in Apple Developer Program and controls App Store Connect; developer gets least-privilege access. | Individual vs organization enrollment, account holder, legal organization details, domain/email, annual fee payer, bundle ID and access grants. |
| D-31 | What native session lifetime, reauthentication and device revocation behavior is acceptable? | Native credentials are stored by the OS and can persist across app restarts; owner/attendant lost-device exposure differs. | Blocks production native authentication configuration; API prototype can use short provisional limits. | Use revocable opaque server-side sessions with finite idle and absolute expiry, OS secure storage and explicit logout; no persistent login beyond expiry. | Idle/absolute duration, reauthentication for sensitive approval actions, concurrent devices, device/session listing and lost-device revocation. |
| D-32 | What are the staging API owner, environment endpoint, synthetic data reset, and tester account process? | Android developer testing must exercise both roles without using production records or shared credentials. | Blocks safe Android acceptance testing; not client skeleton. | Separate staging database, synthetic catalogue and distinct owner/attendant accounts; no production data copy. | Person responsible, reset schedule, test account delivery, data reset approval, network allowlisting and staging cost owner. |
| D-33 | What is the Windows desktop display/browser/accessibility/device baseline? | DPI scaling, screen size, keyboard layout and printer setup determine usable desktop behavior. | Blocks final Windows acceptance criteria; not initial Flutter structure. | Support current Windows 11, keyboard/mouse, scalable text and common 1366×768+ displays; validate on the shop PC. | Actual Windows version, screen resolution/scaling, touch use, language/keyboard, accessibility needs, admin policy and acceptance device. |
